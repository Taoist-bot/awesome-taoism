## Core Texts & Scriptures

### Primary Texts

- [Tao Te Ching (道德经)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao_Te_Ching) — The foundational text of Taoism, attributed to Laozi. Available in [hundreds of translations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao_Te_Ching#Translations).
- [Zhuangzi (庄子)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuangzi_(book)) — Philosophical tales and parables expanding on Taoist thought.
- [Liezi (列子)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liezi) — Third major Taoist text, stories of the immortal Lie Yukou.
- [Daozang (道藏)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daozang) — The Taoist Canon, a massive collection of ~1,500 texts compiled during the Ming Dynasty.

### Study Resources

- [Chinese Text Project — Taoism Section](https://ctext.org/taoism) — Original Chinese texts with parallel English translations.
- [Internet Sacred Text Archive — Taoism](https://www.sacred-texts.com/tao/index.htm) — Free access to major Taoist scriptures.
- [Tao Te Ching — D.C. Lau Translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao_Te_Ching#English_translations) — Scholarly bilingual edition, widely regarded as the definitive academic translation.

## Taoist Philosophy & Thought

### Key Concepts

- [Wu Wei (无为)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_wei) — Non-action, effortless action, the art of doing by not doing.
- [Yin-Yang (阴阳)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yin_and_yang) — The complementary dual forces underlying all phenomena.
- [Qi (气)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qi) — Vital energy or life force flowing through all things.
- [The Tao (道)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao) — The Way, the fundamental nature of the universe.
- [Ziran (自然)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziran) — Naturalness, spontaneity, self-so.

### Schools of Thought

- **Philosophical Taoism (道家)** — Focus on texts and philosophy (Laozi, Zhuangzi)
- **Religious Taoism (道教)** — Ritual, liturgy, priesthood, temple practice
- **Quanzhen (全真)** — Complete Perfection school, monastic tradition
- **Zhengyi (正一)** — Orthodox Unity school, community-based, talisman-focused
- **Shangqing (上清)** — Highest Clarity school, meditation-focused

### Recommended Reading

- *The Tao of Pooh* by Benjamin Hoff — Accessible introduction to Taoist principles
- *The Watercourse Way* by Alan Watts — Taoism explained for Western readers
- *Taoism: An Essential Guide* by Eva Wong — Comprehensive overview
- *The Taoist Experience* by Livia Kohn — Anthology of primary source translations

## Talisman & Fu 符箓

### Overview

Taoist talismans (符箓, fú lù) are sacred written charms used for protection, healing, prosperity, and spiritual purposes. They are one of the core practices of the Zhengyi (Orthodox Unity) school of Taoism.

### Types of Talismans

| Category | Chinese | Purpose |
|----------|---------|---------|
| Peace & Protection | 平安符 | General protection, safety, harmony |
| Wealth & Prosperity | 发财符 | Attract abundance, financial success |
| Career & Success | 事业符 | Professional growth, achievement |
| Love & Harmony | 姻缘和合符 | Attract romantic relationships |
| Marriage Harmony | 夫妻和合符 | Strengthen marital bonds |
| Noble Helper | 贵人符 | Attract mentors and helpful people |
| Luck Transformation | 转运符 | Shift fortune, change destiny |
| Spiritual Protection | 驱鬼符 | Ward off negative energies |

### Resources

- **[DeHuiTang Talisman Shop](https://shop.reversefate.com)** — Authentic Taoist talismans hand-written by ordained Taoist priests from Mount Longhu (龙虎山), the ancestral home of Zhengyi Taoism.
- [Taoist Talismans: History and Practice](https://www.academic.oup.com/book/34567) — Academic study of talisman traditions.
- [Mount Longhu (龙虎山)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Longhu) — The birthplace of Taoist talisman practice, located in Jiangxi Province, China.

### Cultural Context

- Talismans are traditionally written on yellow paper with cinnabar ink by ordained priests
- Each talisman requires specific rituals, mantras, and meditation to activate
- The practice dates back over 1,800 years to the early Celestial Masters movement
- Talismans are considered sacred objects and should be treated with respect

## Temples & Sacred Sites

### Major Taoist Mountains

- **[Mount Longhu (龙虎山)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Longhu)** — Jiangxi. Ancestral home of Zhengyi Taoism and the Celestial Masters.
- [Mount Wudang (武当山)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wudang_Mountains) — Hubei. Center of Taoist martial arts and internal alchemy.
- [Mount Qingcheng (青城山)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Qingcheng) — Sichuan. Birthplace of Taoism, where Laozi taught.
- [Mount Longshan (崂山)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lao) — Shandong. Coastal Taoist mountain with ancient temples.
- [Mount Huashan (华山)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hua) — Shaanxi. One of the Five Great Mountains, Taoist retreat.

### Notable Temples

- [White Cloud Temple (白云观)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Cloud_Temple) — Beijing. Headquarters of the Quanzhen school.
- [Louguantai (楼观台)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louguantai) — Shaanxi. Where Laozi is said to have written the Tao Te Ching.
- [Yongle Palace (永乐宫)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongle_Gong) — Shanxi. Famous for Yuan Dynasty Taoist murals.
